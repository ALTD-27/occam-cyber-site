Dark web exposure: what it means and what to do first

When people say your company is "on the dark web", they usually mean something specific: usernames, passwords or other data that belongs to you is being traded or shared by criminals. Here is how that happens, and what to do first.

What exposure looks like

  • Credentials from other breaches. Staff use their work email on other sites. When one of those sites is breached, the details end up in criminal hands.
  • Malware that steals from devices. Some malware collects saved passwords and login sessions from an infected computer and sends them to the attacker.
  • Leaked or stolen data. Documents and customer records published or offered for sale after an attack on you or a supplier.
  • Access for sale. Some criminals specialise in getting into organisations and selling that access to others.

How it becomes an incident

A reused password lets an attacker simply try it against your email and cloud accounts. A stolen login session can let them in without being asked for a second factor at all, because the session already proved who the user was. From there the usual next steps are reading email, redirecting payments, or preparing something worse.

This is also why business email compromise is so common: the attacker does not need to break anything if they can sign in as someone.

What to do first

  1. Find and end password reuse. Require unique passwords, and use a password manager so people can.
  2. Turn on multi-factor authentication everywhere, and use stronger methods than text messages where you can.
  3. When you suspect exposure, end the sessions as well as changing the password. A changed password does not always end a stolen session.
  4. Treat an infected device as a reason to reset everything used on it, not only the account you noticed.
  5. Check public breach notification services for your domain, so you hear about known leaks.

What ongoing monitoring adds

The steps above deal with what you already know. Monitoring looks for what you do not: criminal forums, marketplaces and leak sites are watched for your staff credentials, your data and your executives, so you find out in days rather than months. A person then decides what to do about it, and you are told what was found and what to do next.
