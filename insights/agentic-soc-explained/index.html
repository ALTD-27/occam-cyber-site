INSIGHTS / SECURITY OPERATIONS

What is an agentic SOC?

A security operations centre, or SOC, is the team and tooling that watches for attacks and responds to them. In an agentic SOC, AI agents do the first pass on every alert, and a named person stays accountable for the decisions.

The problem it is trying to solve

Security tools produce more alerts than people can read. Most turn out to be harmless, but each one has to be checked, and the dull checking is where delays and mistakes creep in. Organisations without a large security team feel this most, because they run the same tools as everyone else with far fewer people.

What the agents do

  • Pull the logs and signals around an alert.
  • Work out who and what is involved: the account, its owner, and the data it can reach.
  • Raise every plausible explanation, then test each one against the evidence.
  • Propose a response, and write down why.

That testing step is Occam's razor in practice: rule out what the evidence rules out, and go with the simplest explanation left standing. You can watch a simulated investigation to see it run.

What people still do

Agents work inside limits you set in writing. Anything beyond those limits, such as isolating a machine or resetting a password, waits for a named person to approve it. That person is accountable for the decision and for the service. A person also decides what the limits should be in the first place.

What it is not

  • It is not a system that acts without limits.
  • It is not a reason to stop having people. Judgement, accountability and the conversation with your leaders stay human.
  • It is not a promise that nothing will get through. No security service can promise that.
  • It is not the same as cover at all hours. Hours of human cover are agreed up front, and a good provider tells you plainly what happens outside them.

Questions to ask any provider

  • What can the agents do without asking, and who set that limit?
  • Who is the named person, and can I speak to them?
  • Where is my data held, including by the models that read it?
  • What evidence do I get for each decision?
